# Corpus inclusion rule: legitimacy

Declared before the search. The screener applies this rule and nothing else.

## Include

A paper is in the corpus if it does work on the construct of legitimacy itself. That means it does at least one of:

- defines or redefines legitimacy
- proposes or revises its types, dimensions or components
- sets its boundaries against neighboring constructs (reputation, status, trust, social approval, institutional pressure, isomorphism)
- changes or argues about its level of analysis (individual judgment, organization, field, society)
- reviews, takes stock of, or critiques the concept itself

## Exclude

A paper is out if it only:

- uses legitimacy as a variable, antecedent, outcome or mediator in a model without engaging its meaning
- treats legitimacy as a background assumption of institutional theory
- studies legitimation tactics or strategies in a setting without revising what legitimacy is
- measures legitimacy with an existing instrument in a new sample

## Borderline

- A paper that builds a new measurement scale is in only if it argues the scale reflects a revised understanding of the construct. Otherwise out.
- A review of institutional theory with a section on legitimacy is in only if that section revises or critiques the concept. Otherwise out.
- Papers on legitimacy of things other than organizations (regimes, institutions, technologies, ventures) are in if they revise the concept in a way that bears on organizational legitimacy, otherwise out.

## Time window

1975 onward. No end date.

## Language

English only.

## Amendment 2026-09-29

Meyer and Rowan 1977 and DiMaggio and Powell 1983 are reclassified from ground truth to context in `seeds.csv`. Both screening models excluded DiMaggio and Powell under this rule, with the reason that it treats legitimacy as a background driver of isomorphism rather than defining it, and that reading is correct. The two papers remain in the ground truth timeline as the pre history of the construct and remain backward chaining sources, but they are not expected members of the corpus and the seed check no longer requires them.
